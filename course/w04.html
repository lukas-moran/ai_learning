The question

A model is evaluated on a future use case, not on how elaborate its code looks. Start by defining the target, information available at prediction time, and the population on which success matters.

Concepts and derivation

Prediction estimates an unknown output for an input; inference（统计推断） investigates relationships and their uncertainty（不确定性）. The same regression fit may serve either goal, but good predictive accuracy alone does not establish an interpretable causal coefficient. State which goal is primary before choosing metrics.

Training data fits parameters. Validation data selects among procedures. A final test set estimates the performance of a frozen procedure. Random splitting is appropriate only when it matches the intended sampling structure: repeated patients require group-aware separation, and forecasting requires respecting time.

For squared error, the best constant on the training set is its mean. Differentiate the sum of squared deviations with respect to the constant, set it to zero, and obtain the sample mean. This gives a baseline with a mathematical reason, not merely a convenient default.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Translate a vague prediction goal into an observable experiment

"Predict house prices" leaves critical details unspecified. Are we predicting a sale price before listing, a final transaction price after inspection, or a neighborhood average? Define a prediction unit, a prediction time, an outcome horizon and an information set. A feature（特征） is legitimate only if it would be known at that moment. A future inspection result may be strongly associated with price while being unavailable to the intended user.

Let X denote available inputs and Y the target. A supervised model estimates a relationship useful for future observations drawn under an intended use scenario. Prediction（预测） asks whether it forecasts well; inference（推断） asks what can be concluded about relationships or parameters under assumptions. Causal claims require additional identification assumptions. Good predictive performance does not turn an arbitrary coefficient into an intervention effect.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Derive the constant baselines instead of treating them as ritual

For squared error, choose c to minimize the sum of (y_i-c)^2 over training observations. Differentiating gives 2n(c-mean(y)), so the minimizer is the training mean. For absolute error, the objective（目标函数） changes slope when c crosses an observation. A median balances observations on either side and minimizes the absolute deviations. Different losses imply different optimal summaries.

A baseline is evaluated on the same held-out observations as the model. Calculate its constant using training outcomes only. A validation mean uses the answers to improve its own prediction and is therefore an invalid deployment baseline. The baseline exposes whether additional complexity adds predictive value under the chosen loss; it also checks the scoring pipeline.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

The argument in mathematical notation

Decompose a constant squared-error objective

\sum_i(y_i-c)^2=\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2+n(c-\bar y)^2

Expand around the training mean. The cross term vanishes because centered observations sum to zero, so the training mean minimizes squared error.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Separate reducible prediction error from outcome noise

Write Y=f(X)+epsilon, with conditional mean-zero noise. For a fixed trained predictor g, expand (Y-g(X))^2 into (f(X)-g(X))^2, epsilon^2 and a cross term. Conditional mean-zero noise makes the expected cross term vanish when the predictor does not use that new outcome noise. Expected squared prediction error then contains an approximation/estimation term and a noise term. More flexible models cannot predict fresh independent noise.

If the fitted predictor itself changes across training samples, its expected squared deviation from f at a fixed x can be decomposed into squared bias and variance（方差）. Bias measures the difference between the average fitted prediction and f(x); variance measures the spread of fitted predictions around that average. This decomposition describes a distribution of fits, not a label assigned to one model merely by looking at its architecture.

ISLP uses this perspective to explain why a more flexible method can reduce training error yet fail to improve test error. Connect it to the Handbook's model examples by generating repeated small training samples, fitting each model and inspecting the spread of predictions at one fixed x. Keep this separate from repeatedly evaluating the same fixed model on the same fixed data.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Train and evaluate two legitimate constant predictors

  1. Training targets are [1,2,9]. Their mean is 4 and their median is 2. Hold out [3,5] without using it to choose either constant.
  2. The mean predictor gives validation squared errors [1,1], so MSE=1. The median predictor gives [1,9], so MSE=5.
  3. For absolute error, c=4 gives absolute errors [1,1], MAE=1; c=2 gives [1,3], MAE=2. On these held-out values, the training mean also wins under MAE.
  4. The training median minimizes training absolute error, not every possible validation realization. An optimal training statistic does not guarantee it wins on one small future sample. State the optimization domain when describing optimality.
详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

A split is a simulation of future use

Use a time split when predicting future periods, a group split when predicting new entities, and a random split only when exchangeability of observations makes that simulation credible. A random split of visits can put one patient in both training and validation, allowing memorized patient characteristics to help. It can answer a different question from generalizing to unseen patients.

After splitting, learn imputation, scaling and feature selection on the training partition. Transform validation with those stored rules. Hand-built transformations based on future outcomes remain leakage（信息泄漏） even if implemented outside a library pipeline. Pipeline structure helps enforce the order but cannot decide whether each feature was available in the first place.

详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Keep fitting and scoring separate

train_y = [1., 2., 9.]
validation_y = [3., 5.]
mean_predictor = sum(train_y) / len(train_y)
median_predictor = sorted(train_y)[len(train_y)//2]
def mse(actual, constant):
    return sum((y-constant)**2 for y in actual)/len(actual)
def mae(actual, constant):
    return sum(abs(y-constant) for y in actual)/len(actual)
assert mse(validation_y, mean_predictor) == 1
assert mse(validation_y, median_predictor) == 5
assert mae(validation_y, mean_predictor) == 1
assert mae(validation_y, median_predictor) == 2
print(mean_predictor, median_predictor)

详细讲解 · 第 4 学习日

Diagnose the tempting shortcuts

Repeat the calculation after moving one extreme observation between training and validation. The purpose is to see sampling variability, not to select the most flattering split. Fix the final protocol before comparing candidate models. Record the split rule, seed when applicable, target definition, metric and baseline.

Handbook examples are excellent for learning fit/predict interfaces; ISLP explains why training error is optimistic for model assessment; CS229 adds a formal learning perspective. These views meet in one experiment only when they use the same prediction task and evaluation distribution. Before moving to regression, ensure a second person could reproduce your baseline without asking what a row or a target means.

详细讲解 · 第 5 学习日

Further practice with worked solutions

  1. Why does a training-optimal mean sometimes lose on validation?

    查看逐步解析

    It minimizes empirical squared loss on the training targets. Validation is a different finite sample; sampling variation and distribution shift（分布偏移） can change the observed ranking.

  2. Can a random seed make a bad evaluation protocol valid?

    查看逐步解析

    No. A seed reproduces a random procedure. It does not repair patient overlap, future information, an unsuitable target or an inappropriate test distribution.

Worked example

  1. Training labels are [1,3], so the constant prediction is 2. Training MSE is (1+1)/2=1.
  2. Validation labels are [4,6]. Predicting 2 gives MSE=(4+16)/2=10. The unit is the square of the label unit.
  3. If all four labels determine the constant, it becomes 3.5 and apparent validation MSE becomes (0.25+6.25)/2=3.25.
  4. That smaller number is contaminated because validation labels helped fit the rule. Improvement in a reported score can be evidence of a worse evaluation procedure.

Comparing the sources

ISLP supplies the prediction/inference and test-error framework. The Handbook shows the estimator fit/predict interface. CS229 describes empirical risk: an average loss over observed examples. Keep the population risk, the training average and a validation estimate conceptually separate.

D2L makes the same split operational in training loops. A held-out set remains held out only if its labels do not affect preprocessing, parameter fitting or the choices being assessed. Repeated validation use is model selection, not final testing.

Implementation

Save split indices and a data identifier. Fit the constant using training labels only; call the same predictor on each split.

Write an MSE function（函数） and test it on the example. Check label and prediction shapes before subtraction.

List any feature that could be created after the outcome. Exclude it if it would be unavailable at the intended prediction time.

import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
X = rng.normal(size=(100, 2))
y = X @ np.array([2., -1.]) + 3 + rng.normal(0, .3, 100)
order = rng.permutation(len(y))
train, val, test = order[:60], order[60:80], order[80:]
assert not (set(train) & set(val) or set(train) & set(test) or set(val) & set(test))
mean_train = y[train].mean()
mse = lambda truth, pred: np.mean((truth - pred)**2)
print("split sizes:", len(train), len(val), len(test))
print("train mean:", mean_train)
print("validation baseline:", mse(y[val], mean_train))
# Deliberately wrong comparison: using validation labels to fit a baseline.
leaked_mean = y[np.r_[train, val]].mean()
print("leaked validation result (invalid):", mse(y[val], leaked_mean))
print("difference in fitted mean:", leaked_mean - mean_train)
# Test labels are intentionally not evaluated here.

Controlled experiment

Reproduce the clean and deliberately leaked mean baselines with the same split. Label the leaked result as a demonstration, never as an improvement.

In the lab, move validation labels while retaining the training-only rule: its prediction should not change.

Try a time-ordered or grouped example on paper and explain why a random row split could answer the wrong question.

Five mastery checks

  1. Why compare with a mean predictor?

    查看参考答案

    It is the training-optimal constant under squared loss and exposes whether features add useful signal.

  2. What selects hyperparameters?

    查看参考答案

    Validation or an inner resampling procedure, not the final test set.

  3. Does fitting a scaler on all X avoid leakage because no y is used?

    查看参考答案

    No. It uses held-out feature distribution information; fit learned preprocessing within training folds.

  4. Does prediction prove causation?

    查看参考答案

    No. Association can predict without identifying an intervention effect.

  5. What must be frozen?

    查看参考答案

    Data protocol, preprocessing, model selection rule and metric before final testing.

One-page recap

A baseline, a split and a metric form the smallest credible learning experiment.

supervised learning（监督学习）: Learning a prediction rule from input-output examples.

baseline（基线）: A deliberately simple reference rule evaluated under the same protocol.

data leakage（数据泄漏）: Use of information unavailable under the intended prediction or evaluation protocol.

generalization（泛化）: Performance beyond the observations used for fitting or selection.

Linear regression will improve the constant by allowing predictions to depend on features.
