# Error Analysis and Claims Proportional to Evidence

Once results exist, the main risk is telling a stronger story than the experiment supports. Analyze differences, failure modes and uncertainty before writing the conclusion.

## Concepts

Match runs by the conditions deliberately held constant and compute a difference for each pair. For higher-is-better metrics use variant minus baseline; for losses define and label the direction. A mean can hide sign changes, so retain individual values and explain variability.

Repeated seeds on a fixed dataset do not create independent samples from the deployment population. They can characterize initialization or training variability under that dataset, while uncertainty from data sampling remains. Confidence intervals and significance tests require assumptions about independence and the resampling unit.

Error analysis examines examples, classes and subgroups to propose mechanisms. A few selected failures are illustrative, not a prevalence estimate. Keep subgroup sample sizes visible and avoid choosing only favorable slices after inspecting results. A follow-up hypothesis should be evaluated with an appropriate new protocol.

## Detailed study

## Error analysis should identify a testable failure mechanism

Start with the cases a fixed model gets wrong or assigns large loss. Group them by plausible mechanisms: missing inputs, rare classes, low image contrast, a time period or a target range. Record counts as well as rates. A subgroup with one failure out of two differs in evidential strength from one with 500 failures out of 1000, even though both display 50%.

Exploratory subgroup analysis（探索性分组分析） helps form a new hypothesis, but searching many groups and reporting only the worst one introduces selection effects. Separate the analysis that suggested a model change from the independent observations used to evaluate that change. Error examples are a diagnostic tool, not an unlimited license to inspect and tune against the final test set.

An ablation（消融实验） removes or changes one component while holding the rest of the protocol as consistent as possible. It asks what the component contributes in that setup. Changing data augmentation, architecture and update budget at once measures a bundle of changes. That can be a legitimate package comparison, but it cannot attribute the gain to one ingredient.

## Paired comparisons preserve information about the same cases

Evaluate two models on the same held-out observations and compare their losses per observation. For error indicators, the key cases are discordant pairs（不一致配对）: model A is right while B is wrong, or vice versa. Cases both get right or both get wrong do not explain the difference in error counts. A paired design often gives a clearer comparison than two unrelated accuracy percentages.

For uncertainty estimates, preserve the sampling unit. An observation-level bootstrap assumes the resampled observations represent independent sampling units; grouped or time-dependent data need an appropriate alternative. A confidence interval depends on the procedure and assumptions. It is not a probability that a fixed fitted model is "true", and it does not remove dataset shift or selection bias.

## The argument in mathematical notation

### A paired comparison identifies the net difference

$
\widehat\Delta=\frac1n\sum_i(\ell_{A,i}-\ell_{B,i})
$

Evaluate both models on the same cases. Positive difference favors B under this loss sign convention.

## Distinguish random-seed variability from dataset uncertainty

Suppose repeated training seeds on one fixed split give error rates [0.10,0.11,0.09]. Their spread describes sensitivity to the training randomness included in those runs. It does not by itself measure how performance would change if a new population sample were collected, because the dataset stayed fixed.

Now fix one trained model and evaluate it on several independent comparable samples. Their variation concerns evaluation sampling. If the model is refitted on each sample, both training-sample and evaluation variability can enter. Name what is repeated, what is held fixed and what population your uncertainty statement refers to.

When comparing two methods, use a paired protocol where possible: common splits, compatible seeds or data orders, and per-case losses. Record all runs. Do not turn the smallest error across seeds into a typical performance claim unless seed selection is explicitly part of a procedure evaluated on independent data.

## Inspect what an apparent accuracy improvement consists of

1. On 100 shared cases, both models are correct on 80, both wrong on 10, A alone correct on 4 and B alone correct on 6. A has 84% accuracy and B has 86%.

2. Only ten cases distinguish their correctness. B improves six cases but loses four, for a net gain of two cases. The 2-point improvement should be read alongside those counts.

3. The average paired error difference error_A-error_B is (6-4)/100=0.02. Positive means B makes fewer errors under this sign convention.

4. Investigate the ten discordant examples. If the gain is concentrated in one rare pattern, the next hypothesis can target that mechanism, but confirm it using development data and a fresh evaluation protocol rather than repeated final-test tuning.

## Use a run table that preserves negative results

Record each candidate's configuration, seed, selection rule, primary metric, runtime and failure status. Do not remove divergent runs from a summary without reporting their exclusion. When comparing random seeds, pair corresponding data splits and initialization policies when meaningful. Distinguish variability from random training runs on one dataset from uncertainty about new datasets; they are different sources of variation.

## Calculate a paired error difference from shared outcomes

```python
a_correct = [True]*80 + [False]*10 + [True]*4 + [False]*6
b_correct = [True]*80 + [False]*10 + [False]*4 + [True]*6
only_a = sum(a and not b for a,b in zip(a_correct,b_correct))
only_b = sum(b and not a for a,b in zip(a_correct,b_correct))
acc_a = sum(a_correct)/len(a_correct)
acc_b = sum(b_correct)/len(b_correct)
paired_difference = (only_b-only_a)/len(a_correct)
assert only_a == 4 and only_b == 6
assert abs(acc_b-acc_a-paired_difference) < 1e-12
print(acc_a, acc_b, only_a, only_b, paired_difference)
```

## Keep the strength of the conclusion proportional to the design

A positive paired difference on one split is evidence about those observations under that protocol. A stronger claim about average future performance requires more information about sampling and variability. Inspect whether the effect is practically meaningful, whether computation costs changed, and whether an important subgroup worsened. A winning overall number does not answer all deployment questions.

When reading a paper, separate the proposed mechanism, the comparison design and the reported observations. Reproduce one claim with a manageable budget instead of chasing the full headline result. ISLP's resampling and uncertainty ideas help evaluate evidence; CS229's learning framework clarifies assumptions; the practical resources supply implementations that must still follow the experimental design.

## Further practice with worked solutions

### Why inspect discordant examples rather than only total accuracy?

Solution: They show which observations account for the difference. The same net accuracy gain can arise from very different tradeoffs and failure mechanisms.

### Does a bootstrap interval correct for repeatedly selecting the best of many test-set variants?

Solution: Not automatically. Resampling a selected result does not erase the selection process. Keep tuning separate from final assessment and account for the actual selection procedure.

## Worked example

1. Baseline accuracies are [0.90,0.92,0.91] and variant accuracies [0.91,0.91,0.93].
2. Paired differences are [0.01,-0.01,0.02], or [1,-1,2] percentage points. Mean difference is 2/3 of a percentage point.
3. The sample standard deviation of these differences is about 1.5275 percentage points. One run is worse, so the variant did not improve every run.
4. These three illustrative runs alone do not establish a statistically reliable population improvement. They describe the observed matched experiments.

## Comparing the sources

ISLP introduces sampling variability and resampling. CS229 discusses generalization and selection. Combine them with complete experiment logs: repeated model runs and resampled datasets answer different questions.

A paper discovered through arXiv or the supplied Papers with Code link can suggest a comparison, but its claims depend on its own datasets and budgets. Replication means matching or explicitly documenting departures from those conditions.

## Implementation

Store one row per run with configuration, seed, split, metric direction and status. Include failed runs.

Compute paired differences only where matching is meaningful. Keep dataset, seed and fold identifiers separate.

Inspect confusion counts and a predefined set of subgroup summaries. Preserve raw predictions for audit.

## Controlled experiment

Use the lab to shift all variant scores and observe the mean difference and count of positive differences.

Write one conclusion supported by the actual result table and one stronger conclusion that it cannot support.

Select a concrete error pattern and design the next controlled experiment; do not rerun indefinitely until a favorable result appears.

## Self-check

1. Is 0.01 accuracy one percent relative improvement?

Answer: It is one percentage point; relative percentage improvement uses the baseline as denominator.

2. Why pair results?

Answer: To compare matched conditions and reduce some irrelevant variation when the matching is justified.

3. Do three seeds prove significance?

Answer: No; inferential claims need justified assumptions and adequate design.

4. Should failed runs disappear?

Answer: No; report failures and the selection rule.

5. What is required?

Answer: All runs, paired differences where valid, bounded error analysis and explicit uncertainty sources.

## One-page recap

A useful conclusion tells readers what changed, under which conditions, and what remains unknown.

The final report connects every claim back to a reproducible artifact.

## Terms

- paired comparison (配对比较): Comparing outcomes under matched conditions by analyzing their differences.
- uncertainty (不确定性): The range of plausible variation or error under a stated source and model.
- percentage point (百分点): An absolute difference between percentages.
