The question

A constant baseline ignores X. Linear regression uses one coefficient per feature（特征） and an intercept, while retaining an objective（目标函数） simple enough to derive and verify end to end.

Linear-regression gradient

L=\frac1n\lVert Xw+b\mathbf1-y\rVert_2^2,\quad \nabla_wL=\frac2nX^\top(Xw+b\mathbf1-y)

Rows are observations; the weight gradient has the same shape（形状） as the weights.

Concepts and derivation

Write predictions as Xw+b with X of shape (n,d), w of shape (d,), and scalar b. Let r=Xw+b-y and L=mean(r squared). Changing w changes every residual（残差） through its corresponding feature row. Applying the chain rule（链式法则） and collecting terms gives gradient_w=2 X-transpose r/n and gradient_b=2 mean(r).

The geometric view（视图） minimizes the length of the residual vector. The probabilistic view assumes independent Gaussian errors with common variance（方差）; maximizing likelihood then gives the same least-squares coefficients. The squared-loss optimization remains meaningful without Gaussian noise, but Gaussian interval and likelihood interpretations require their assumptions.

A gradient check compares an analytic derivative with (L(w+h)-L(w-h))/(2h). Too large h gives approximation error for general functions; too small h magnifies floating-point cancellation. Use several moderate h values, compare relative and absolute error, and test more than one parameter.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Build the regression model from a conditional mean

A simple linear model writes the conditional mean of Y given x as b+wx. The observed outcome is this mean plus an error. The coefficient w is a change in the modeled conditional mean for one unit of x, holding the other included features fixed in a multiple regression. Its interpretation depends on units, included variables and the assumed form. It is not automatically a causal effect.

Choose squared loss when large residuals should be penalized strongly and the conditional mean is the target. For one observation the derivative of (b+wx-y)^2 with respect to w is 2(b+wx-y)x. A positive residual and a positive x push w downward in a gradient step. Summing these contributions and dividing by n produces the MSE（均方误差） gradient. This explains the sign and factor before writing matrix notation.

With an intercept, minimizing squared error forces the residuals to sum to zero on the fitting data. It does not force held-out residuals to average zero. That distinction becomes useful when diagnosing a shifted validation population. Coefficient estimates can be unstable with correlated predictors even when their combined predictions are stable.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Connect projection, likelihood and optimization without conflating them

Geometry views fitted outcomes as the projection of y onto the span of the design columns. Probability adds an assumption such as independent Gaussian errors with common variance. Taking negative log likelihood then gives a constant plus squared residual sum divided by twice that variance. Both arguments lead to the same coefficient minimizer, but only the probabilistic argument supports Gaussian uncertainty（不确定性） statements under its assumptions.

CS229's introductory half-sum objective and this route's mean-squared objective differ by n/2. Their minimizers agree while their raw gradients differ. ISLP also studies estimated coefficient uncertainty and residual diagnostics. Matching a library's coefficients validates neither the Gaussian assumption nor the claim that an association is causal. Keep computational checks and statistical assumptions in separate paragraphs of the report.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

The argument in mathematical notation

Slope, intercept and conditional Gaussian errors

\hat w=\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2},\quad \hat b=\bar y-\hat w\bar x

For a nonconstant single predictor with an unconstrained intercept, center both variables to obtain the slope.

Likelihood adds assumptions

-\log p(y\mid X,w,b,\sigma^2)=\frac n2\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i(y_i-x_i^\top w-b)^2

This expression assumes independent Gaussian additive errors with common variance and the stated conditional mean.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Use the noise model to interpret a standard error cautiously

For the single-predictor line with independent constant-variance errors and fixed predictor values, the variance of the fitted slope is sigma squared divided by Sxx, where Sxx is the sum of squared centered predictor values. Wider predictor spread supplies more information about slope under this model. More repeated observations at nearly the same x can estimate a local mean well while contributing little information about how the mean changes across x.

In the worked line, n=3 and SSE=2/3. The usual residual variance estimate divides by n-2 because two coefficients were fitted, giving 2/3. Sxx=2, so the estimated slope standard error is sqrt(1/3), about 0.577. This tiny sample has only one residual degree of freedom; an interval would be very uncertain. Do not use a normal approximation casually or interpret these constructed three points as strong evidence.

This is where the three resource perspectives diverge usefully. The Handbook can fit a line, CS229 derives its objective and likelihood, and ISLP develops uncertainty under assumptions. A correct coefficient calculation is the beginning of inference（统计推断）, not its completion. If errors are heteroscedastic or dependent, the usual formula needs reconsideration.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Fit a line with an intercept all the way through

  1. Use x=[0,1,2] and y=[1,2,5]. The means are x_bar=1 and y_bar=8/3. Center x to [-1,0,1] and y to [-5/3,-2/3,7/3].
  2. The sum of centered cross-products is 4 and the sum of squared centered x values is 2. Therefore w=4/2=2 and b=y_bar-w*x_bar=2/3.
  3. Predictions are [2/3,8/3,14/3]. Residuals prediction minus observation are [-1/3,2/3,-1/3]. They sum to zero and their dot product（内积） with x is zero.
  4. SSE is 2/3 and MSE is 2/9. A constant training-mean predictor has SSE=26/3. Adding the slope lowers training SSE here; the size of the validation improvement remains an empirical question.
详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Read a gradient check as a small scientific experiment

Perturb only one parameter while holding all others fixed. Calculate the loss at plus h and minus h, divide their difference by 2h, and compare against the corresponding analytic derivative. Check the intercept too. Test several points, because a sign bug can accidentally disappear at an optimum. Near a zero derivative, absolute error is more informative than a relative ratio with a nearly zero denominator.

详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Fit the small line and verify its stationarity

x, y = [0., 1., 2.], [1., 2., 5.]
xm, ym = sum(x)/len(x), sum(y)/len(y)
w = sum((a-xm)*(b-ym) for a,b in zip(x,y))/sum((a-xm)**2 for a in x)
b = ym-w*xm
r = [w*a+b-target for a,target in zip(x,y)]
assert abs(w-2) < 1e-12
assert abs(b-2/3) < 1e-12
assert abs(sum(r)) < 1e-12
assert abs(sum(a*e for a,e in zip(x,r))) < 1e-12
assert abs(sum(e*e for e in r)/len(r)-2/9) < 1e-12
print({"slope":w,"intercept":b,"residuals":r})

详细讲解 · 第 4 学习日

Use residuals to ask a specific next question

Add a fourth observation with a large x coordinate and compare the fitted line. An observation far from the predictor mean can have high leverage（杠杆值）: its x position gives it strong potential influence on the slope. A large residual and high leverage describe different properties. Inspect both before deciding whether a point is an error, a rare valid case or evidence against the linear form.

Compare a straight line with a quadratic feature expansion under identical splits. The quadratic model is nonlinear in x but linear in its coefficients. Its lower training error alone cannot justify choosing it. This experiment connects the geometric freedom of a larger design space with ISLP's bias-variance discussion and prepares regularization（正则化） in W08.

详细讲解 · 第 5 学习日

Further practice with worked solutions

  1. Why is b=y_bar-w*x_bar at a least-squares optimum with an intercept?

    查看逐步解析

    The intercept derivative sets the mean residual to zero: mean(wx+b-y)=0. Rearranging gives the relation. It holds on fitting observations under this unconstrained intercept setup.

  2. Multiply all losses by 100. What happens?

    查看逐步解析

    The minimizer is unchanged, but each gradient is multiplied by 100. Gradient descent with the same numeric learning rate（学习率） can become unstable. Divide the learning rate by 100 to match the original updates.

Worked example

  1. Let x=[1,2], y=[2,4], b=0. Then L(w)=2.5(w-2)^2. At w=0, loss is 10 and derivative is -10.
  2. A step of size 0.1 in the negative derivative direction moves w to 1; loss becomes 2.5.
  3. At w=1 and h=0.1, losses at 1.1 and 0.9 are 2.025 and 3.025. Their central difference is -5, exactly matching the derivative here.
  4. The central difference is exact in exact arithmetic for this quadratic; that is not a universal property of gradient checks.

Comparing the sources

CS229 §1 uses a half-sum squared objective in its introductory derivation. This course uses mean squared error. For n observations, the CS229 objective is n/2 times this loss, so its gradient is n/2 times larger. Match learning-rate scaling before comparing updates.

ISLP emphasizes coefficients, residual behavior and uncertainty; the Handbook demonstrates fitting tools. Neither a library match nor a passing derivative check proves the data assumptions. Verify implementation first, then examine residual patterns and the prediction protocol.

Implementation

Separate predict, loss and gradient. Keep intercept handling explicit rather than inconsistently adding a column of ones.

Check gradients on a tiny deterministic input before training. Use float64 and compare every coefficient and b.

Use a least-squares library reference with matching intercept behavior. Compare predictions and loss; coefficients can be nonunique when features are redundant.

import numpy as np

def loss_grad(X, y, w, b):
    assert y.shape == (X.shape[0],) and w.shape == (X.shape[1],)
    residual = X @ w + b - y
    return np.mean(residual**2), 2 * X.T @ residual / len(y), 2 * residual.mean()

X = np.array([[1., 2.], [2., -1.], [3., 1.]])
y = np.array([2., 4., 5.])
w, b = np.array([.2, -.1]), .3
loss, dw, db = loss_grad(X, y, w, b)
h = 1e-5
numeric = np.zeros_like(w)
for i in range(len(w)):
    step = np.zeros_like(w); step[i] = h
    numeric[i] = (loss_grad(X,y,w+step,b)[0] - loss_grad(X,y,w-step,b)[0]) / (2*h)
numeric_b = (loss_grad(X,y,w,b+h)[0] - loss_grad(X,y,w,b-h)[0]) / (2*h)
assert np.allclose(dw, numeric, atol=1e-7)
assert np.isclose(db, numeric_b, atol=1e-7)
print("loss, analytic, numeric:", loss, dw, numeric)
for h in [1e-2, 1e-5, 1e-12]:
    numeric_b = (loss_grad(X,y,w,b+h)[0] - loss_grad(X,y,w,b-h)[0]) / (2*h)
    print("step and absolute error:", h, abs(db - numeric_b))

Five mastery checks

  1. What shape is gradient_w?

    查看参考答案

    (d,), matching w.

  2. Why does the intercept gradient average residuals?

    查看参考答案

    Every residual has derivative 1 with respect to b.

  3. When does least squares equal Gaussian maximum likelihood?

    查看参考答案

    With independent, constant-variance Gaussian additive errors and the stated linear mean.

  4. Can the same minimizer have different gradients?

    查看参考答案

    Yes: multiplying an objective by a positive constant preserves its minimizer but rescales its gradient.

  5. What passes the gate?

    查看参考答案

    A written derivative, shape assertions, and numerical checks over several inputs and perturbations.

One-page recap

Derive the objective you actually implement. A missing n or factor of 2 affects optimization even when the optimum is unchanged.

gradient（梯度）: The vector of partial derivatives of a scalar function（函数）.

likelihood（似然）: The probability model viewed as a function of parameters for fixed observed data.

finite difference（有限差分）: A derivative approximation obtained by perturbing inputs.

A correct gradient is ready to become a training algorithm.
