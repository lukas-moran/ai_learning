The question

A flexible fit may exploit accidental structure. Regularization modifies the objective so that fitting the observed data is balanced against a preference for simpler parameters.

Concepts and derivation

Use J=MSE+lambda*sum(w squared), excluding the intercept. The penalty contributes 2*lambda*w to the gradient（梯度）. With lambda positive, large coefficients must earn their complexity through reduced data loss. Shrinking coefficients generally increases the best achievable training MSE, but validation error can improve or worsen.

Regularization depends on representation. If one feature（特征） is measured in meters and another in millimeters, equal coefficients do not mean equal effects. Standardize using training statistics when a coefficient penalty should treat feature directions comparably. The intercept sets the overall level and is commonly left unpenalized.

An L1 penalty has corners at zero and can yield exact zeros; L2 usually shrinks continuously. The Bayesian interpretation connects an L2 penalty to a Gaussian prior（先验）, but the prior precision（精确率） also depends on likelihood（似然） scaling and noise variance（方差）. A penalty is not automatically a universal measure of model complexity.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Regularization deliberately changes the fitted problem

When many different functions fit a small training sample well, empirical fit alone may select an unstable solution. Regularization（正则化） introduces a preference: smaller coefficients, fewer active features, smoother functions or another stated restriction. The preference must be justified in the context of units and expected future data. A penalty is not a mechanical guarantee that validation error decreases.

Ridge adds lambda times the squared coefficient norm to the mean data loss. Its gradient contribution is 2*lambda*w. The intercept is usually excluded so shifting all target values does not incur a slope-like penalty. Lasso uses an absolute-value penalty, which has a corner at zero and can yield exact zero coefficients. An ordinary derivative formula at zero is insufficient for lasso; one needs subgradients（次梯度） or an appropriate solver.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Understand shrinkage in one direction before a full matrix formula

For an orthogonal design, squared-error fitting separates into independent coefficient problems. Each ridge coefficient is pulled toward zero, with the amount determined by penalty strength relative to information in that direction. Correlated designs mix these directions; an eigenvalue（特征值） view（视图） explains why poorly informed directions are shrunk more strongly. This connects ridge to conditioning from W02.

Scaling a feature by 100 divides its required coefficient by 100 for the same predictions. The squared coefficient penalty then changes by a factor of 10000. Standardization（标准化） makes a common penalty more comparable across units, although it still encodes a modeling preference. Estimate scaling parameters inside each training fold, not once on the full dataset.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

The argument in mathematical notation

Align mean-loss ridge with sum-loss ridge

\frac1n\lVert Xw-y\rVert^2+\lambda\lVert w\rVert^2\quad\sim\quad\lVert Xw-y\rVert^2+\alpha\lVert w\rVert^2,\quad\alpha=n\lambda

The objectives differ by a positive factor n when alpha=n*lambda, so they have the same minimizer but differently scaled gradients.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Explain why ridge can stabilize a nearly redundant direction

For centered predictors without a penalized intercept, the ridge normal equation for a mean data term is (X-transpose-X+n*lambda*I)w=X-transpose-y. In an eigenvector（特征向量） direction of X-transpose-X with eigenvalue s, adding n*lambda replaces a possibly tiny denominator s with s+n*lambda. A weakly supported direction is suppressed rather than amplified through a near-zero inverse.

When lambda is positive, this matrix is invertible even if X has redundant columns. That does not make the original unpenalized coefficients identifiable（可识别的） from data; it chooses a unique solution using the additional penalty preference. Distinguish data information from the regularizer（正则项）'s contribution.

A Gaussian-prior interpretation connects this choice to CS229's probabilistic perspective: a zero-centered coefficient prior discourages large values. Its numerical strength depends on the likelihood variance and loss normalization（归一化）. ISLP then asks how to choose the strength for prediction through resampling. The implementation must align these definitions before a coefficient comparison becomes meaningful.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Solve a scalar ridge problem and compare conventions

  1. Let x=[1,2], y=[2,4], no intercept, and objective mean((wx-y)^2)+lambda*w^2. The data term is 2.5(w-2)^2.
  2. Differentiating gives 5(w-2)+2*lambda*w=0, so w=10/(5+2*lambda). At lambda=0 it is 2; at lambda=2.5 it is 1.
  3. The penalty reduces the coefficient magnitude but increases training MSE from 0 to 2.5 at lambda=2.5. That is the intended tradeoff, not a failed optimizer.
  4. A sum-of-squares formulation SSE+alpha*w^2 is equivalent here when alpha=n*lambda=5. Comparing alpha=2.5 with lambda=2.5 would compare different problems.
详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Separate objective alignment from hyperparameter selection

First verify that a library and your implementation use the same data reduction, penalty, intercept treatment and feature scaling. Only then compare coefficients. Next select the penalty using validation or inner cross-validation（交叉验证）, keeping the final test set unused. Coefficient equality under an aligned objective is an implementation check; a chosen lambda's predictive quality is an evaluation result.

Verify the scalar ridge optimum and library scaling

x, y = [1., 2.], [2., 4.]
lam = 2.5
n = len(x)
w = sum(a*b for a,b in zip(x,y))/(sum(a*a for a in x)+n*lam)
alpha = n*lam
w_sum = sum(a*b for a,b in zip(x,y))/(sum(a*a for a in x)+alpha)
assert w == 1
assert w_sum == w
gradient = 2*sum((w*a-b)*a for a,b in zip(x,y))/n + 2*lam*w
assert abs(gradient) < 1e-12
print({"lambda":lam, "alpha":alpha, "weight":w})

详细讲解 · 第 4 学习日

Read a regularization path as a change in assumptions

Fit a grid ranging from nearly unpenalized to strongly shrunk models on fixed folds. Plot coefficient magnitudes, training error and validation error against penalty strength. A falling coefficient norm accompanied by increasing training error is expected. A U-shaped validation curve is possible but not guaranteed in a finite experiment. Preserve the actual curve instead of drawing the textbook shape（形状） by assumption.

ISLP frames tuning through the bias-variance tradeoff; CS229 can connect an L2 penalty to a Gaussian prior; D2L discusses weight decay（权重衰减） in optimization. A prior interpretation needs its probability model and scaling. Coupled L2 gradients and decoupled optimizer weight decay are not interchangeable for every optimizer. W19 returns to that distinction.

详细讲解 · 第 5 学习日

Further practice with worked solutions

  1. Why exclude the intercept from a standard ridge penalty?

    查看逐步解析

    It permits a target offset to be represented without shrinking the overall response level toward zero. Penalizing it is possible, but defines a different problem and must be aligned across implementations.

  2. Can zero lasso coefficients establish that a feature has no relationship with the outcome?

    查看逐步解析

    No. Selection depends on the sample, correlated predictors, scale, penalty and model class. A zero estimated coefficient is a property of this fitted procedure, not a universal independence statement.

Worked example

  1. For J=(w-3)^2+lambda*w^2, setting the derivative to zero gives w=3/(1+lambda).
  2. At lambda=0 the optimum is 3; at lambda=1 it is 1.5. The latter has data loss 2.25 and penalty 2.25.
  3. The smaller coefficient does not prove a better prediction on future observations. That requires a valid evaluation.
  4. For n=100, minimizing SSE+alpha*||w||^2 matches mean squared error+lambda*||w||^2 when alpha=100*lambda, assuming identical preprocessing and intercept treatment.

Comparing the sources

ISLP Ch. 6 emphasizes selecting penalty strength with resampling. CS229 relates regularization to a prior. D2L discusses weight decay in optimization. These explanations meet only after the loss reduction and coefficient conventions are aligned.

scikit-learn Ridge uses a sum-of-squares objective plus alpha times squared norm. This course uses a mean data term. Comparing identical numeric alpha and lambda without accounting for n compares different optimization problems.

Implementation

Add the penalty to both reported objective and gradient. Report unpenalized prediction error separately.

Select lambda using validation or cross-validation, with a scaler inside each training fold.

Keep the lambda=0 model as a reference. Document whether b is penalized and whether the data term is summed or averaged.

Reference program

import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
X = rng.normal(size=(120, 20))
y = 2*X[:, 0] - X[:, 1] + rng.normal(0, 1.5, 120)
Xtr, Xv, ytr, yv = X[:35], X[35:], y[:35], y[35:]
mu, scale = Xtr.mean(0), Xtr.std(0)
scale[scale == 0] = 1
Z = (Xtr-mu)/scale; Zv = (Xv-mu)/scale
ym = ytr.mean(); centered = ytr-ym
for lam in [0., .1, 1., 10.]:
    # Objective: mean squared error + lam * ||w||^2; intercept not penalized.
    w = np.linalg.solve(Z.T @ Z + len(Z)*lam*np.eye(Z.shape[1]), Z.T @ centered)
    train_mse = np.mean((Z @ w + ym-ytr)**2)
    val_mse = np.mean((Zv @ w + ym-yv)**2)
    print("lambda, norm, train MSE, val MSE:", lam, np.linalg.norm(w), train_mse, val_mse)
# Only the validation table may be used to select lambda.

Five mastery checks

  1. What gradient does lambda*||w||^2 add?

    查看参考答案

    2*lambda*w.

  2. Does shrinkage guarantee better validation error?

    查看参考答案

    No; it trades fit against a restriction that may help or hurt.

  3. Why exclude b from the penalty?

    查看参考答案

    A common convention is to regularize feature effects without shrinking the baseline（基线） level toward zero.

  4. When does alpha=n*lambda apply?

    查看参考答案

    When comparing the stated SSE and MSE formulations with identical remaining conventions.

  5. What must be recorded?

    查看参考答案

    Reduction, intercept policy, scaling fit range, chosen strengths and validation-only selection.

One-page recap

A regularization parameter has meaning only together with its objective, feature scale and evaluation protocol.

regularization（正则化）: A constraint or penalty used to control a fitted procedure.

bias–variance tradeoff（偏差与方差权衡）: The tension between systematic approximation error and sensitivity to sampled training data.

ridge（岭回归）: Squared-error regression with an L2 coefficient penalty.

sparsity（稀疏性）: A representation with many coefficients exactly zero.

Resampling makes model comparison more systematic without removing uncertainty（不确定性）.
