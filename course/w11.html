The question

Two classifiers can assign identical labels while placing their boundaries at different distances from observations. SVMs make that geometric separation part of the objective（目标函数）.

Concepts and derivation

Use labels -1 and +1 and score s=x dot w+b. Correct classification requires y*s>0; satisfying the unit functional margin requires y*s>=1. The geometric signed distance is y*s/||w||. Rescaling both w and b changes the functional margin but not the boundary or geometric distance.

A soft-margin objective combines a coefficient norm with penalties for margin violations. In the convention 0.5*||w||^2+C*sum(hinge), a larger C places more weight on violations relative to coefficient size. This differs from a convention using mean loss plus lambda regularization（正则化）; parameter comparisons require translating the whole objective.

Kernels replace feature-space inner products with a function（函数） of original inputs. A nonlinear boundary then becomes possible without explicitly constructing all mapped coordinates. This is a computational and geometric idea; it does not remove tuning or scaling requirements. The full dual derivation is an extension, not a prerequisite for checking a linear pipeline.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

A margin measures separation in feature-space units

For binary labels y in {-1,+1}, a linear score is f(x)=w-transpose-x+b. Correct classification requires y*f(x)>0. Scaling both w and b by ten changes score magnitudes without changing the boundary. Therefore the functional margin y*f(x) alone is not a geometric distance. Divide by the norm of w to measure signed distance to the separating hyperplane（分离超平面）.

A hard-margin SVM fixes the functional-margin scale with constraints y_i*f(x_i)>=1, then minimizes one half the squared weight norm. Under that normalization（归一化）, shrinking the norm enlarges the geometric margin. This explains why the norm term is central: it resolves an otherwise arbitrary scale and encodes the preference for separation.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Soft margins trade violations against a simple boundary

Real data may not be perfectly separable. Hinge loss（合页损失） max(0,1-y*f(x)) is zero for correctly classified cases beyond the unit functional margin, positive for nearby correct cases and larger for misclassified ones. A soft-margin objective combines a norm penalty and C times a sum of hinge losses. Increasing C makes violations more expensive relative to the norm penalty under that convention.

A kernel（核函数） computes an inner product in an implicit feature space. It can produce nonlinear boundaries in the original inputs while retaining a linear rule in that feature space. A valid positive-semidefinite kernel is more than an arbitrary similarity score. Its scaling, feature scaling and hyperparameters affect geometry, so compare complete objectives and preprocessing before comparing C values.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

The argument in mathematical notation

Soft-margin objective and geometric distance

\min_{w,b}\ \frac12\lVert w\rVert^2+C\sum_i\max(0,1-y_i(w^\top x_i+b)),\quad d_i=\frac{y_i(w^\top x_i+b)}{\lVert w\rVert}

This convention uses summed hinge loss. Its C is not automatically equivalent to a coefficient used with mean hinge loss.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Feature expansion and kernels express the same kind of inner-product reasoning

Define an explicit map phi(x1,x2)=[x1^2,sqrt(2)*x1*x2,x2^2]. Its dot product（内积） between x and z is x1^2*z1^2+2*x1*x2*z1*z2+x2^2*z2^2, which equals (x dot z)^2. Thus the degree-two polynomial kernel computes an inner product in this expanded feature space without constructing the three entries explicitly.

For x=[1,2] and z=[3,4], the ordinary dot product is 11 and the kernel value is 121. Computing the feature-map dot product gives 9+48+64=121 as well. The expansion makes a linear boundary in transformed coordinates correspond to a nonlinear boundary in original coordinates.

The kernel identity is a statement about representation. It does not eliminate regularization, hyperparameter（超参数） choice or evaluation. Scaling inputs changes this geometry and can drastically alter an RBF or polynomial kernel. Use CS229's derivation to understand the implicit space, the Handbook to inspect the resulting boundary and ISLP to assess the selected procedure.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Separate geometric margin from a correct class decision

  1. Let w=[1,0], b=0. The boundary is x1=0 and the weight norm is one. A positive example at x=[2,3] has functional margin 2 and distance 2, so its hinge loss is zero.
  2. A positive example at [0.5,3] is correctly classified but has margin 0.5 and hinge loss 0.5. Correctness does not mean it lies beyond the desired margin.
  3. A negative example at [0.5,3] has signed margin -0.5 and hinge loss 1.5. Its label conflicts with the side of the boundary.
  4. Multiply w and b by two. The boundary and geometric distances stay unchanged, but functional margins and hinge losses change. The norm penalty also changes. SVM optimization balances those quantities under a fixed convention.
详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Relate score outputs to probability outputs explicitly

An SVM decision score is not a probability. A large score indicates position relative to the learned boundary under its scale. Probability calibration（校准） requires a separate fitting and evaluation procedure. Do not compare raw SVM score magnitudes with logistic probabilities. For a practical experiment, use an established SVM solver and reserve the hand calculation for checking margins and hinge losses.

详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Verify margins and scale invariance of distance

from math import sqrt
w, b = [1., 0.], 0.
points = [([2.,3.],1), ([0.5,3.],1), ([0.5,3.],-1)]
norm = sqrt(sum(a*a for a in w))
hinges = []
for x,y in points:
    score = sum(a*c for a,c in zip(w,x))+b
    distance = y*score/norm
    doubled_distance = y*(2*score)/(2*norm)
    assert distance == doubled_distance
    hinges.append(max(0., 1-y*score))
assert hinges == [0., 0.5, 1.5]
print(hinges)

详细讲解 · 第 4 学习日

Run a two-factor investigation without mixing the factors

First vary C for a linear SVM while holding standardized inputs and folds fixed. Then, in a separate comparison, introduce an RBF kernel and tune its width inside development data. Varying kernel width, C and preprocessing simultaneously makes a boundary change hard to interpret. Show decision regions or a small margin table along with held-out performance.

The Handbook makes boundary geometry visible, CS229 derives constraints and duality（对偶性）, and ISLP connects the classifier to assessment. Use the derivation to understand which observations can affect the boundary. Then use the held-out experiment to assess whether that boundary serves your task. Full dual optimization and KKT derivations remain a separate advanced route after this operational understanding.

详细讲解 · 第 5 学习日

Further practice with worked solutions

  1. Can a correctly classified observation have positive hinge loss?

    查看逐步解析

    Yes. A point with 0<y*f(x)<1 is on the correct side but inside the unit functional margin.

  2. Why is a kernel matrix required to satisfy more than symmetric similarity?

    查看逐步解析

    The standard kernel construction represents inner products in a feature space and requires positive semidefiniteness. Arbitrary symmetric similarities need not have that property.

Worked example

  1. For y=+1 and s=0.25, the prediction is positive but hinge loss is 0.75.
  2. For s=2 the loss is zero. For s=-0.5 the loss is 1.5. Correct classification is weaker than satisfying the margin.
  3. If w=[3,4] and score is 2, geometric distance is 2/5=0.4. Doubling w and b doubles the score and norm, leaving this distance unchanged.
  4. With C=2 the penalty for the first example is 1.5 before adding the norm term.

Comparing the sources

ISLP Ch. 9 builds the margin intuition. CS229 §§5–6 connects kernels, functional/geometric margins and optimization duality. Boyd and Vandenberghe supply the optional convex-duality foundation.

A library estimator may use hinge or squared hinge and may sum or average penalties. LinearSVC and a linear-kernel SVC are not guaranteed to optimize identical conventions or treat the intercept（截距） identically. Compare settings and predictions before interpreting coefficient differences.

Implementation

Build StandardScaler followed by a linear SVM inside one Pipeline. Fit the scaler only on training folds.

Document label encoding, penalty type, C and intercept treatment. Plot signed scores separately from predicted labels.

Keep the kernel extension small: one additional model with a declared tuning budget, not an unrestricted search against the test set.

from sklearn.datasets import load_iris
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.svm import SVC

X,y = load_iris(return_X_y=True)
mask = y != 0; X,y = X[mask],y[mask]
Xtr,Xv,ytr,yv = train_test_split(X,y,test_size=.3,stratify=y,random_state=42)
for C in [.1, 1., 10.]:
    model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(),SVC(kernel="linear",C=C))
    model.fit(Xtr,ytr)
    print("C/train/validation:",C,model.score(Xtr,ytr),model.score(Xv,yv))
for margin in [-1.,.2,2.]:
    print("signed margin, hinge:",margin,max(0.,1-margin))

One-page recap

Always state which margin and which objective convention you mean.

margin（间隔）: A signed measure of separation from a decision boundary.

hinge loss（合页损失）: max(0,1-y*s) for a signed label y and score s.

kernel（核函数）: A function representing an inner product in a feature space.

duality（对偶性）: A relationship between an optimization problem and a related bound-generating problem.

The same geometry supports dimensionality reduction without using labels.
