# From Tables to Defensible Observations

Before fitting a model, learn what a row means. A table combines values with labels, types, units and missingness. Cleaning changes the statistical object you are analyzing.

## Concepts

Pandas aligns operations by index labels; NumPy normally aligns by positions and shapes. Two Series with the same length may not add as expected if their labels differ. Verify row identity after joins, and check whether a join unexpectedly multiplies rows. A clean-looking table can already contain duplicated evidence.

A missing value is not a zero. Deleting incomplete rows can change the population represented by the sample. Mean imputation keeps rows but compresses variance and ignores uncertainty. Estimate an imputation rule on training data only when the table feeds a predictive pipeline.

A plot supports a bounded observation: a distribution is skewed, two measured variables co-vary, or a subgroup has different values. A scatterplot does not identify a causal effect. Label axes, units, aggregation choices and sample counts so that readers can assess what was actually compared.

## Detailed study

## A table encodes an observational design

Start a data audit by completing the sentence "one row is ...". One row might be a person, a visit, a transaction or a sensor reading. If one patient contributes five visits, five rows are not five independent patients. A table can be perfectly rectangular while violating the evaluation design you intend. Preserve entity identifiers long enough to detect repeated measurements and grouping requirements.

A data dictionary（数据字典） should record meaning, units, allowed range, missing-value convention and availability time. A column called score could be an input available at prediction time, an outcome recorded later, or a code for a category. Numeric storage does not make a variable quantitative. Averaging postal codes has no useful distance interpretation.

Pandas aligns Series by labels. When two tables are merged, cardinality matters: a one-to-one join preserves one row per entity, whereas repeated keys on both sides create combinations. Assert key uniqueness when that is part of the data contract. Sorting or resetting an index can conceal alignment mistakes rather than repair them.

## Missingness and aggregation change the population being described

Consider recording exercise hours only for people who return a questionnaire. The observed mean estimates the mean among respondents unless assumptions connect respondents to everyone else. Dropping missing rows may make a plot cleaner while changing its population. Mean imputation replaces uncertainty with a fixed value and usually compresses the observed distribution; it is a modeling choice, not recovered information.

Aggregation can also reverse an association. Suppose clinic A treats mostly severe patients while clinic B treats mostly mild patients. An overall outcome comparison mixes treatment quality with case mix. Report subgroup counts alongside rates and ask whether the grouping variable preceded the outcome. ISLP's distinction between prediction and inference makes these questions explicit; the Handbook's grouping and visualization tools let you inspect them.

## The argument in mathematical notation

### The unit of analysis appears in the denominator

$
\bar y_{\mathrm{visits}}=\frac{\sum_g m_g y_g}{\sum_g m_g},\qquad \bar y_{\mathrm{patients}}=\frac1G\sum_g y_g
$

If patient g contributes m_g visits with the same measurement, the visit mean weights that patient m_g times.

## Compare group rates before and after mixing populations

Consider two methods evaluated on easy and hard cases. Method A succeeds on 81 of 90 easy cases and 2 of 10 hard cases, with rates 0.9 and 0.2. Method B succeeds on 19 of 20 easy cases and 24 of 80 hard cases, with rates 0.95 and 0.3. B has a higher success rate in both groups.

Overall A succeeds on 83 of 100 cases, while B succeeds on 43 of 100. The reversal occurs because A was evaluated mostly on easy cases and B mostly on hard cases. The overall rates answer questions about different case mixtures. Comparing them as though case difficulty were controlled confounds the method comparison with case mix.

Reweight both methods to a common 50/50 mixture: A's rate becomes 0.5*0.9+0.5*0.2=0.55, and B's becomes 0.625. This is a descriptive standardization, not automatic causal identification. We still need to know how methods and difficulty were assigned and whether the chosen groups capture relevant differences. A grouped table with denominators is essential evidence that a single overall chart would hide.

## A duplicate-key join that quietly reweights an average

1. A patient table contains (A,10) and (B,20), with mean measurement 15. A visits table contains patient keys [A,A,B].

2. Joining visits to patients produces measurements [10,10,20]. The mean is now 40/3, approximately 13.33. No measurement value changed; the unit of analysis changed from patients to visits.

3. If the question is the average patient measurement, aggregate to one row per patient or retain the original patient table. If it is the average measurement per visit, the joined mean may be the intended statistic.

4. Write both the numerator and denominator in words. That usually exposes the ambiguity sooner than inspecting the decimal result.

## Choose a plot by the comparison it must support

Use a histogram for a distribution, a scatterplot for paired quantitative observations, and a dot or bar plot for category summaries. A bar height representing a rate should be accompanied by its sample count. Match bins and axes across groups when their shapes are being compared. Start with a readable static plot before adding interactive controls. A visualization is evidence only when readers can determine what was counted and how it was transformed.

## Audit the unit of analysis before plotting

```python
patients = {"A": 10., "B": 20.}
visits = ["A", "A", "B"]
patient_mean = sum(patients.values()) / len(patients)
visit_values = [patients[key] for key in visits]
visit_mean = sum(visit_values) / len(visit_values)
assert patient_mean == 15
assert abs(visit_mean - 40/3) < 1e-12
counts = {key: visits.count(key) for key in patients}
assert counts == {"A": 2, "B": 1}
print({"patient_mean": patient_mean, "visit_mean": visit_mean,
       "visits_per_patient": counts})
```

## Make the cleaning decision visible in the result

Construct a table with five observed values and two missing entries. Compare complete-case deletion with an explicit imputation rule. Report row count, missing count, mean, spread and any subgroup imbalance under both choices. Fit a learned imputation rule only on training observations when using it for prediction. For descriptive analysis, say exactly which population and cleaning policy the summary describes.

A useful audit concludes with three observations and a limitation. For example, "the median waiting time is larger in group A among recorded visits" is narrower and more defensible than "membership in A causes delay". Reproducibility includes the transformations that produced the plotted table, not merely the plotting command.

## Further practice with worked solutions

### Why can a join change a mean without changing any original measurement?

Solution: Repeated join keys change how often measurements contribute. That changes weights and often the unit of analysis. Check cardinality and report the denominator.

### A missing-income indicator predicts an outcome. What should be checked?

Solution: Check why and when income becomes missing, whether that mechanism will persist at deployment, and whether the indicator reveals information collected after the prediction time. Predictive association alone does not resolve those issues.

## Worked example

1. Observed values [1,2,3] have mean 2 and population variance 2/3. A fourth value is missing.
2. Fill it with 2: the mean stays 2 but population variance becomes (1+0+1+0)/4=1/2. Preserving a mean does not preserve a distribution.
3. For the three observed values, sample variance with denominator n-1 is 1. The different denominator is a convention with estimation assumptions, not a rounding difference.
4. If Series a has labels [A,B] and b has [B,A], addition pairs A with A and B with B. Inspect labels before converting to an unlabeled array.

## Comparing the sources

The Handbook provides selection, grouping, joins and plotting techniques. ISLP asks what population, response and predictors those operations represent. Use the first for execution and the second to question the analysis design.

Handbook examples may use older pandas APIs. Preserve the intended operation and consult current pandas documentation for its supported form. An API migration should not silently change missing-value treatment or row alignment.

## Implementation

Build a data dictionary with units, type, missing count and intended role for each column. Inspect a few raw rows before summarizing.

Create one distribution plot and one relationship plot with labels. Show group sample sizes when aggregating.

Assert row counts and key uniqueness before and after merging. Keep raw data separate from transformed data.

## Controlled experiment

Compare deletion with one explicit imputation rule on the same tiny table. Record mean, variance and row count before and after.

Use the lab to change the imputed value. Explain why both the center and spread can move.

Write three observations supported by your plots and one tempting causal claim the plots cannot justify.

## Self-check

1. Why is NaN not zero?

Answer: It represents absence, whereas zero is an observed value.

2. Does mean imputation preserve variance?

Answer: No. In the worked example it reduces population variance from 2/3 to 1/2.

3. Why check join cardinality?

Answer: Duplicate keys can multiply rows and unintentionally reweight observations.

4. When may a plot support causation?

Answer: Only with an appropriate identification design and assumptions, not from visual association alone.

5. What is the deliverable?

Answer: A reproducible data audit with dictionary, two labeled plots and bounded conclusions.

## One-page recap

Labels, missingness and units are part of the data. Preserve them long enough to detect errors that numeric arrays cannot reveal.

## Terms

- dataframe (数据框): A labeled two-dimensional table whose columns can have different types.
- missingness (缺失机制): How and why values are absent.
- confounding (混杂): A common cause or related variable that distorts a naive association.
