# K-means and the Limits of Unsupervised Structure

PCA changes coordinates; clustering assigns observations to groups. Project two combines them while keeping their objectives separate.

## Concepts

K-means minimizes the sum of squared distances from each observation to its assigned center. Holding centers fixed, choose the closest center. Holding assignments fixed, differentiate a cluster sum of squares to obtain its mean as the best center. These two steps do not increase the objective when performed exactly.

The objective is not jointly convex in centers and assignments. Different initializations can lead to different local solutions. Empty clusters need an explicit policy, ties need a deterministic rule, and a stopping criterion must prevent endless iteration. Cluster numbers are identifiers, not recovered class labels.

A Gaussian mixture uses soft membership probabilities. EM computes responsibilities and then updates parameters under those weights. K-means resembles a hard-assignment limit of restricted spherical mixtures, but it is not the same objective as a general mixture likelihood. Soft assignments express a model-specific uncertainty, not an automatic guarantee of true categories.

## Detailed study

## K-means alternates between two conditional minimizations

K-means chooses assignments and centers to minimize within-cluster squared Euclidean distance. If centers are fixed, each observation chooses its closest center. If assignments are fixed, each nonempty cluster's mean minimizes its squared distances. Alternating these two operations cannot increase the stated objective when each operation is performed exactly. It need not find the global optimum.

The mean update follows the same derivative as the constant squared-loss baseline in W04. This is a useful conceptual reuse: K-means is repeatedly solving that small problem within groups whose membership is also being estimated. Empty clusters require an explicit recovery policy because their mean is undefined. Ties also require a deterministic or documented rule.

## An optimization objective does not define meaningful categories

Squared Euclidean distance treats units and scale as part of similarity. A feature measured in thousands can dominate another measured in fractions. Standardization changes the clustering objective's practical meaning. Features that encode arbitrary category numbers should not automatically enter Euclidean distance as though their differences were quantitative.

Spherical compact clusters are favored by the centroid objective. Curved groups, unequal densities and outliers can produce partitions that disagree with a human grouping. Inertia（簇内平方距离和） usually decreases as k increases, so choosing the k with the smallest training inertia is not a sufficient selection rule. Stability, application meaning and held-out or domain checks complement the optimization score.

## The argument in mathematical notation

### Alternate assignment and center estimation

$
J=\sum_i\lVert x_i-\mu_{c_i}\rVert^2,\quad c_i\leftarrow\arg\min_k\lVert x_i-\mu_k\rVert^2,\quad\mu_k\leftarrow\frac{\sum_{i:c_i=k}x_i}{n_k}
$

The update requires a nonempty cluster. Each step minimizes the same objective conditional on the other variables.

## A probabilistic extension explains what K-means leaves out

Consider a mixture of k Gaussian components with equal spherical covariance sigma squared times I and equal prior weights. For one observation, each component's density decreases exponentially with squared distance to its center. Its posterior responsibility（后验责任度） is a normalized exponential over those negative distances. All centers can receive a nonzero responsibility, unlike a hard K-means assignment.

As the common variance approaches zero while centers remain distinct, the nearest center dominates those relative weights. This limiting connection explains why hard assignments can resemble a restricted mixture model. It does not make every K-means fit a fully estimated Gaussian mixture: ordinary K-means does not estimate arbitrary covariances, and its distortion objective is not the general mixture likelihood.

The EM algorithm（期望最大化算法） alternates a posterior-weight calculation with parameter updates maximizing an expected complete-data objective. This is a route beyond the main chapter once conditional probability and mean updates are secure. Use it to compare assumptions, not merely to replace one library call with a more complicated one.

## Perform a complete assignment and update cycle

1. Use one-dimensional data [0,1,9,10] and initial centers [0,9]. Distances assign [0,1] to the first and [9,10] to the second. Initial SSE is 0+1+0+1=2.

2. Update the means to [0.5,9.5]. Squared distances are now four copies of 0.25, so SSE becomes 1.

3. Reassignment leaves the two groups unchanged; the next mean update does too. The procedure has reached a fixed point for this initialization.

4. If labels 0 and 1 are exchanged, the partition and objective remain the same. Cluster identifiers are arbitrary names. Never count a label permutation as a different scientific grouping.

## Check objective monotonicity with the correct state

Log the objective after a full assignment/update cycle using the matching centers and assignments. Mixing old assignments with new centers can confuse comparisons. Stop on stable assignments, small center movement or a stated iteration budget. Use established implementations for full experiments and use the tiny loop to understand one cycle. For comparisons across restarts, retain the seed, initialization method and final objective.

## One transparent K-means cycle

```python
points = [0.,1.,9.,10.]
centers = [0.,9.]
labels = [min(range(2), key=lambda j: (x-centers[j])**2) for x in points]
old_sse = sum((x-centers[j])**2 for x,j in zip(points,labels))
new_centers = [sum(x for x,j in zip(points,labels) if j==k)/labels.count(k)
               for k in range(2)]
new_sse = sum((x-new_centers[j])**2 for x,j in zip(points,labels))
assert new_centers == [0.5,9.5]
assert old_sse == 2 and new_sse == 1
assert new_sse <= old_sse
print(labels, new_centers, old_sse, new_sse)
```

## Further practice with worked solutions

### Why can K-means converge yet produce an unsatisfactory partition?

Solution: It can settle at a local optimum, or its squared-distance objective can be poorly matched to the application. Algorithmic convergence and scientific usefulness are different questions.

### What should happen if a cluster has no observations?

Solution: Its mean is undefined. Use an explicit recovery policy such as reinitializing a center, document it and include that event in the run log.

## Worked example

1. Take points [0,2,8,10] and centers [0,10]. Nearest-center assignment gives groups [0,2] and [8,10].
2. Initial squared-distance sum is 0+4+4+0=8. Updating centers gives [1,9].
3. The new sum is 1+1+1+1=4, and reassignment leaves the groups unchanged.
4. Relabeling centers as cluster 1 and cluster 0 changes no geometry. If both centers start at 0, a deterministic tie rule can leave one cluster empty.

## Terms

- clustering (聚类): Grouping observations according to a chosen similarity criterion.
- centroid (质心): The mean of observations assigned to a cluster.
