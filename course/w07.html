The question

Regression used an unrestricted real output. Binary classification asks for a probability and then a decision. Those are two different objects, with different ways to assess them.

Concepts and derivation

Logistic regression sets z=x dot w+b and p=1/(1+exp(-z)). The binary cross-entropy is -y log(p)-(1-y)log(1-p). Differentiating through the sigmoid simplifies the derivative with respect to z to p-y. Thus the mean-loss weight gradient is X-transpose(p-y)/n. A threshold turns probabilities into actions; changing it changes decisions without refitting probabilities.

A discriminative（判别式） model estimates P(y|x) directly. A generative model（生成式模型） specifies P(x|y) and P(y), then uses Bayes rule: the posterior（后验） is proportional to likelihood（似然） times prior（先验）. Gaussian discriminant analysis with a shared covariance（协方差） gives a linear log-odds boundary; naive Bayes（朴素贝叶斯） instead assumes conditional independence of features given the class. These assumptions are substantive, not interchangeable coding tricks.

Extreme logits（类别分数） make naive exponentials overflow. Evaluate binary loss as max(z,0)+log(1+exp(-abs(z)))-y*z, or use a tested logits-based loss. Stable arithmetic preserves the same mathematical objective（目标函数）. Accuracy alone cannot measure how well probabilities represent uncertainty（不确定性）.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Why a classification probability needs a different output map

A linear score z=w-transpose-x+b can be any real number, while a probability must lie between zero and one. Logistic regression uses p=1/(1+exp(-z)). The log odds log(p/(1-p)) equals z, so a one-unit feature（特征） increase adds its coefficient to log odds when other included features are fixed. The same coefficient does not imply a constant change in probability: the sigmoid is steep near zero score and flat at extreme scores.

For a binary outcome, the Bernoulli likelihood is p^y*(1-p)^(1-y). Taking the negative log gives cross entropy（交叉熵）: -y log p-(1-y) log(1-p). A confident wrong prediction is heavily penalized. This differs from counting classification mistakes, which discards probability information and changes discontinuously when a score crosses a threshold.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

Derive the simple gradient and the cost-sensitive decision

The sigmoid derivative is p(1-p). Differentiating the cross entropy through it cancels denominator factors, leaving d loss/dz=p-y. Multiplying by x gives the parameter contribution (p-y)x. The cancellation depends on this compatible probability model and loss, not on a universal rule for all networks. For a batch, average the contributions under the stated reduction.

Probability estimation and decision making are separate. Suppose a false positive costs C_FP and a false negative costs C_FN. Predict positive when C_FP*(1-p)<C_FN*p, which gives p>C_FP/(C_FP+C_FN). A threshold of 0.5 corresponds to equal costs under this simple setup. Calibrated probabilities and a correct cost model are needed to interpret this decision rule.

详细讲解 · 第 1 学习日

The argument in mathematical notation

Logistic likelihood and its score derivative

p=\sigma(z),\quad \ell=\log(1+e^z)-yz,\quad \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z}=p-y

Evaluate the softplus term stably for large scores. A loss derivative in score space still needs the chain rule（链式法则） for parameters.

Cost-sensitive probability threshold

C_{\rm FP}(1-p)<C_{\rm FN}p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p>\frac{C_{\rm FP}}{C_{\rm FP}+C_{\rm FN}}

This simple decision rule assumes the predicted probability and error costs describe the intended use.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Use Bayes rule to distinguish modeling directions

Suppose P(Y=1)=0.2, P(X=1 given Y=1)=0.8 and P(X=1 given Y=0)=0.1. The joint positive mass at X=1 is 0.16 and the negative mass is 0.08. Therefore P(Y=1 given X=1)=0.16/(0.16+0.08)=2/3. A class-conditional model plus a prior has produced a conditional prediction.

A logistic model parameterizes P(Y=1 given X) directly instead of separately estimating those ingredients. For a binary X, the two conditional probabilities can be represented by an intercept（截距） and a slope on the log-odds scale. With more complex data, the two modeling approaches impose different restrictions and can have different sample efficiency or robustness. Neither direction is automatically superior.

Now keep the class-conditional probabilities fixed but change the prior to 0.5. The posterior at X=1 becomes 0.8/(0.8+0.1)=8/9. This thought experiment shows why probabilities can change when prevalence changes. Applying that correction in practice requires evidence that the class-conditional behavior really stayed fixed; it is not a universal remedy for every distribution shift（分布偏移）.

详细讲解 · 第 2 学习日

Calculate a probability, a loss, a gradient and a decision

  1. Take one feature x=2, w=0, b=0 and true label y=1. The score is zero, p=0.5 and negative log likelihood is log(2), approximately 0.6931.
  2. The score derivative is p-y=-0.5; the weight derivative is -1 and the intercept derivative is -0.5. With learning rate（学习率） 0.1, w becomes 0.1 and b becomes 0.05.
  3. For the same input, the new score is 0.25 and p is approximately 0.5622. Loss decreases to about 0.5759.
  4. If a false negative costs four times a false positive, the simple cost threshold is 1/(1+4)=0.2. A probability of 0.3 would then lead to a positive decision even though it is below 0.5.
详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Implement loss from the score, not from a rounded probability

Computing log(sigmoid(z)) directly is fragile for large magnitude scores. Use the equivalent score-based expression max(z,0)-y*z+log(1+exp(-abs(z))). Both exponential and logarithm then operate in safer ranges. A probability rounded to zero may hide a finite score and create an infinite log loss. Match the library's input contract: some APIs expect probabilities and others expect logits（未归一化分数）.

详细讲解 · 第 3 学习日

Stable binary loss and an explicit gradient step

from math import exp, log1p, log
def binary_loss(z, y):
    return max(z, 0.) - y*z + log1p(exp(-abs(z)))
def sigmoid(z):
    return 1/(1+exp(-z)) if z >= 0 else exp(z)/(1+exp(z))

x, y, w, b = 2., 1., 0., 0.
p = sigmoid(w*x+b)
w -= 0.1*(p-y)*x
b -= 0.1*(p-y)
assert abs(binary_loss(0, 1)-log(2)) < 1e-12
assert binary_loss(w*x+b, y) < binary_loss(0, y)
assert abs(binary_loss(1000, 0)-1000) < 1e-12
assert abs(binary_loss(-1000, 1)-1000) < 1e-12
print(w, b, sigmoid(w*x+b), binary_loss(w*x+b, y))

详细讲解 · 第 4 学习日

Compare logistic regression with a generative explanation

CS229 also approaches classification through class-conditional distributions and Bayes' rule. A generative model（生成式模型） describes how features are distributed within each class and combines this with class priors. Logistic regression instead models the conditional class probability directly. Different assumptions can yield similar-looking decision boundaries while estimating different quantities along the way.

In a small experiment, keep the same split and compare probability quality and thresholded decisions separately. Changing only the threshold leaves log loss unchanged because estimated probabilities are unchanged. It changes the confusion matrix（混淆矩阵） and costs. This is why an ISLP evaluation discussion complements a Handbook fit/predict example and a CS229 likelihood derivation.

详细讲解 · 第 5 学习日

Further practice with worked solutions

  1. Why is a probability increase smaller near p=0.99 than near p=0.5 for the same small score change?

    查看逐步解析

    The sigmoid derivative p(1-p) is about 0.0099 at 0.99 and 0.25 at 0.5. Score changes translate differently depending on the current probability.

  2. Does lowering a decision threshold improve the probability model?

    查看逐步解析

    No. It changes the decision rule, generally increasing positive predictions. Probability estimates and log loss stay the same; recall（召回率）, precision（精确率） and cost can change.

Worked example

  1. At z=0, p=0.5. With y=1 the loss is log(2), approximately 0.6931, and the derivative with respect to z is -0.5.
  2. At z=log(3), p=0.75. A threshold of 0.5 predicts 1; a threshold of 0.8 predicts 0. The probability stays 0.75.
  3. Suppose P(y=1)=0.2 and an observation is three times as likely under class 1 as class 0. Posterior odds are 3 times prior odds: 3*(0.2/0.8)=0.75.
  4. Posterior probability is 0.75/(1+0.75)=3/7, below 0.5. A likelihood ratio（似然比） above one does not by itself imply a positive decision.

import numpy as np

def sigmoid(z):
    return np.exp(-np.logaddexp(0, -z))

def objective(X, y, w):
    z = X @ w
    return np.mean(np.logaddexp(0, z) - y*z), X.T @ (sigmoid(z)-y) / len(y)

rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
X = np.column_stack([rng.normal(size=(100, 2)), np.ones(100)])
y = (X[:, 0] - X[:, 1] > .2).astype(float)
w = np.zeros(3)
loss, analytic = objective(X, y, w)
h = 1e-5
numeric = []
for i in range(3):
    step = np.eye(3)[i]*h
    numeric.append((objective(X,y,w+step)[0] - objective(X,y,w-step)[0])/(2*h))
assert np.allclose(analytic, numeric)
for _ in range(300):
    _, grad = objective(X[:70], y[:70], w)
    w -= .2*grad
p = sigmoid(X[70:] @ w); truth = y[70:]
for threshold in [.3, .5, .7]:
    pred = p >= threshold
    tp = np.sum(pred & (truth == 1)); fp = np.sum(pred & (truth == 0))
    fn = np.sum(~pred & (truth == 1)); tn = np.sum(~pred & (truth == 0))
    print("threshold, TP FP FN TN:", threshold, tp, fp, fn, tn)
print("extreme logits:", sigmoid(np.array([-1000.,0.,1000.])))

Five mastery checks

  2. Does changing the decision threshold change cross-entropy?

    查看参考答案

    No, if the probability estimates remain fixed.

  3. Does a 0.8 probability mean 80% accuracy on one item?

    查看参考答案

  4. Why can generative and discriminative boundaries agree?

    查看参考答案

One-page recap

A score becomes a probability through a model and a decision through a policy. Do not collapse all three into one number.

logit（对数几率分数）: The real score log(p/(1-p)) corresponding to a probability p.

cross-entropy（交叉熵）: A loss measuring the negative log probability assigned to the observed outcome.

generative model（生成式模型）: A model of how observations and labels are jointly distributed.

Bayes rule（贝叶斯公式）: The identity relating conditional probabilities through their joint probability.

Regularization changes how strongly the data can move the fitted coefficients.
