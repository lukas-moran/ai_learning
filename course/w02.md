# Linear Systems, Geometry, and Numerical Trust

Array shapes describe how a calculation is allowed to run. Linear algebra describes what it means, and numerical analysis asks whether a small input error can change the answer substantially.

## Concepts

The columns of A are images of the coordinate directions. Multiplying Ax combines those columns with weights from x. Solving Ax=b asks whether b can be represented that way, and whether that representation is unique. A square full-rank matrix has one solution for each b; a singular matrix can have none or infinitely many.

A residual tests substitution, not stability. Even a nearly exact equation solution may change greatly after a small change in b when A is ill-conditioned. Distinguish the conditioning of the mathematical problem from the stability of the algorithm used to solve it.

Least squares extends the idea to overdetermined systems by minimizing squared residual length. It does not require the residual to vanish. Later, regression will attach statistical meaning to this geometric problem; geometry alone does not establish noise assumptions or causal interpretation.

## Detailed study

## A matrix is a collection of images of basis directions

Take A=[[2,1],[0,1]]. Its first column says that the first coordinate direction maps to (2,0); its second says that the second direction maps to (1,1). Multiplying A by (u,v) gives u times the first column plus v times the second. Matrix multiplication becomes easier to remember when read as composition of maps: applying B and then A gives AB, so the inner dimensions must describe the same intermediate space.

Solving Ax=b reverses this question. Is b in the span（张成空间） of A's columns? If so, do those columns give a unique representation? Two identical columns can be traded against one another, producing multiple coefficient vectors for the same output. This is the geometric reason redundant features make regression coefficients nonunique. Numerical output alone cannot determine whether the model parameters are identifiable（可识别的）.

## Why least squares creates orthogonality

For more equations than unknowns, an exact solution may not exist. Choose x to minimize the squared length of Ax-b. If the residual still has a component in a column direction of A, a small move in the corresponding coefficient can reduce it. At a minimum, the residual is orthogonal to every column, giving A-transpose times (Ax-b)=0. Rearranging yields the normal equations. This is a stationarity argument, not an instruction to form an inverse.

A full-column-rank A gives a unique least-squares coefficient vector. Without that condition, fitted values are still unique but coefficients need not be. QR and SVD solve the same geometric problem without explicitly squaring the conditioning through A-transpose-A. MML supplies the geometry, CS229 connects it to regression, and numerical library documentation determines the implemented solver.

## The argument in mathematical notation

### Least-squares stationarity

$
L(x)=\lVert Ax-b\rVert_2^2,\quad \nabla L=2A^\top(Ax-b),\quad A^\top Ax=A^\top b
$

At a minimum the residual is orthogonal to the column space. Use a numerical least-squares solver rather than forming an inverse.

## A singular design can still have unique fitted values

Suppose a design has two identical columns [1,2]. For coefficient vector [a,b], its fitted vector is [a+b,2(a+b)]. Only the sum a+b affects predictions. If the target is [3,6], every vector with a+b=3 fits perfectly: [3,0], [1,2] and [-10,13] are examples. A solver choosing one of them is applying an additional convention rather than discovering a uniquely determined scientific decomposition.

The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse（摩尔-彭若斯广义逆） selects the minimum Euclidean-norm coefficient vector among least-squares solutions. Here that is [1.5,1.5]. To see why, write b=3-a: a^2+(3-a)^2=2(a-1.5)^2+4.5. The minimum occurs at 1.5. The fitted values remain [3,6] for all exact solutions.

MML's independence and decomposition concepts therefore clarify a statistical warning: an unstable or arbitrary coefficient need not imply an equally unstable prediction. Conversely, reliable predictions do not establish that individual feature effects are identified. Report whether your claim concerns fitted values, parameters or both.

## Find the closest constant to three inconsistent measurements

1. Fit a single constant c to observations 1,2,6. Then A is a column of three ones and b=[1,2,6]. The objective is (c-1)^2+(c-2)^2+(c-6)^2.

2. Its derivative is 2[(c-1)+(c-2)+(c-6)]=6c-18. Setting it to zero gives c=3.

3. The residual Ax-b is [2,1,-3]. It is not zero, but its dot product with the column [1,1,1] is zero. The squared residual length is 14.

4. Check a nearby constant 3.1: the squared residual length becomes 14.03. The extra 0.03 equals three times the squared displacement 0.1^2. This follows because the cross term vanishes at the mean.

## Residual, forward error and conditioning answer different questions

A solver residual tells us how well the computed solution satisfies the supplied equations. Forward error compares that solution with the exact solution to the intended problem. A small residual may coexist with a large forward error when small input perturbations are strongly amplified. Record units and relative scales before declaring an error small. A difference of 0.01 is tiny for one measurement and enormous for another.

## Verify the orthogonality condition directly

```python
observed = [1., 2., 6.]
constant = sum(observed) / len(observed)
residuals = [constant-y for y in observed]
sse = sum(r*r for r in residuals)
assert constant == 3
assert sum(residuals) == 0
assert sse == 14
shift = 0.1
shifted = sum((constant+shift-y)**2 for y in observed)
assert abs(shifted - sse - len(observed)*shift**2) < 1e-12
print(constant, residuals, sse, shifted)
```

## Design a sensitivity experiment with an interpretable direction

Use A=diag(epsilon,1) and b=[0,1]. The exact solution is [0,1]. Perturb b to [delta,1], and the first solution coordinate becomes delta/epsilon. The input difference has length delta and the solution difference has length delta/epsilon. Reducing epsilon while keeping delta fixed exposes the fragile direction. This is more informative than adding unrelated random noise to every entry.

Now repeat with perturbation [0,delta] instead. There is no amplification in the second coordinate. A condition number bounds worst-case sensitivity; it does not predict equal amplification for every direction. In your report, distinguish the direction tested from the worst-case guarantee.

## Further practice with worked solutions

### If two columns of A are identical, why can coefficients be nonunique?

Solution: Adding t to one coefficient and subtracting t from the other leaves Ax unchanged. Predictions can be identified even when individual coefficients are not.

### Does an orthogonal residual mean observations are statistically independent?

Solution: No. Orthogonality here is an algebraic property of a fitted vector. Independence is a property of a joint probability distribution and requires separate assumptions.

## Worked example

1. For A=[[2,1],[1,3]] and b=[5,7], the equations give x=[1.6,1.8]. Substitution returns [5,7].
2. For A=diag(0.1,1), b=[1,1], the solution is [10,1]. Perturb only b1 to 1.01: x becomes [10.1,1].
3. The absolute change in x1 is 0.1 even though the absolute input change is 0.01. Both systems can have zero residual.
4. The 2-norm condition number is 10 for this diagonal matrix. It is a worst-case relative sensitivity measure, not a claim that every perturbation attains a tenfold relative error.

## Comparing the sources

MML develops span, independence and projections; NumPy supplies solve and lstsq. Use solve for a square full-rank system and lstsq for a least-squares problem. A symbolic inverse explains a map, but forming an inverse is usually unnecessary for solving one system.

CS229 derives normal equations for regression. Forming X-transpose-X can square the condition number in the full-column-rank case. This explains why practical least-squares routines often use QR or SVD even when a textbook presents the normal-equation identity.

Answer: A unique solution for every b is impossible; a particular b may yield no solution or infinitely many.

Answer: Yes: each perturbed problem can be solved exactly while its solution changes substantially.

## Terms

- rank (秩): The number of independent directions represented by a matrix.
- residual (残差): The discrepancy Ax-b after substituting a candidate solution.
- condition number (条件数): A measure of worst-case sensitivity of a problem to relative perturbations.
